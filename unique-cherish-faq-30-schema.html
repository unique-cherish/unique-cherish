🛡️ Allergy & Skin Safety (Q1–Q8)

Q1: What does "hypoallergenic" mean for earrings?

Hypoallergenic means the earrings are designed to minimize the risk of allergic reactions. For stainless steel jewelry, this specifically means the material releases very low levels of nickel — the most common metal allergen. True hypoallergenic earrings use 316L stainless steel (surgical-grade), which contains molybdenum that locks nickel atoms into the alloy and prevents them from leaching onto your skin. Our earrings comply with the EU EN1811 nickel release standard of less than 0.5 μg/cm²/week.

Q2: Can I wear these earrings if I have a nickel allergy?

Yes. Our earrings are made from 316L stainless steel, which is specifically chosen for its extremely low nickel release rate. In EN1811 testing, 316L releases less than 0.02 μg/cm²/week — well below the 0.5 μg/cm²/week threshold known to trigger reactions in nickel-sensitized individuals. Over 99% of people with nickel allergies can wear 316L without any reaction. If you have an extremely rare sensitivity to molybdenum or chromium (the other elements in 316L), consult a dermatologist before ordering.

Q3: Why do some earrings make my ears itch or turn green?

Earrings cause itching or green discoloration for two main reasons: (1) Nickel allergy — when nickel ions leach from the metal and contact your skin, triggering an immune response (itching, redness, rash). This is common with low-grade stainless steel (304), brass, or nickel-plated jewelry. (2) Copper oxidation — when copper or brass jewelry reacts with sweat and skin oils, it forms copper salts that can leave a green mark on your skin. Our 316L stainless steel earrings are PVD-coated, creating a dense barrier that prevents both nickel release and metal-skin reactions.

Q4: Is 316L stainless steel safe for newly pierced ears?

316L stainless steel is widely used for initial piercing jewelry by professional piercers worldwide. It meets the ASTM F138 standard for surgical implant materials and is approved by the Association of Professional Piercers (APP). The low carbon content (≤0.03%) and molybdenum addition make it resistant to corrosion in the moist, healing environment of a fresh piercing. However, always follow your piercer's specific aftercare instructions and wait until your piercing is fully healed before changing jewelry.

Q5: What's the difference between 316L and 304 stainless steel for sensitive ears?

The critical difference is molybdenum. 316L contains 2-3% molybdenum, which serves as a "nickel lock" — it binds nickel atoms tightly within the metal matrix, preventing them from leaching out when exposed to sweat. 304 stainless steel lacks molybdenum, so nickel can release more freely, especially in warm, humid conditions. In EN1811 testing, 316L releases less than 0.02 μg/cm²/week while 304 can release 0.05–0.25 μg/cm²/week. For daily-wear earrings, 316L is the clear choice for sensitive ears.

Q6: What if I react even to 316L stainless steel?

True allergic reactions to genuine 316L stainless steel are extremely rare (affecting less than 1% of the population). If you experience a reaction, consider these possibilities: (1) The item may not be genuine 316L — some sellers label 304 or lower grades as "surgical steel" without specifying the grade. (2) You may have a sensitivity to molybdenum or chromium (extremely rare — consult a dermatologist for patch testing). (3) The reaction may be to something on the surface (cleaning residue, polishing compound) rather than the metal itself. Try thoroughly washing the earrings before first wear. If the reaction persists, discontinue use and consult a healthcare professional.

Q7: Are PVD-coated earrings safe for sensitive skin?

Yes, PVD coatings are extremely skin-safe for two reasons: (1) The coating materials — titanium nitride (gold-tone), zirconium nitride (rose gold-tone), and chromium nitride — are the same biocompatible materials used in medical implants and surgical instruments. They are chemically inert and do not react with skin. (2) The PVD coating creates an additional physical barrier between the base metal and your skin — even if you have a sensitivity to the base metal, the PVD layer prevents contact. Combined with a 316L stainless steel base, PVD-coated earrings offer dual-layer protection for sensitive skin.

Q8: Do your earrings contain lead, cadmium, or other toxic metals?

No. Our earrings are made from 316L stainless steel with PVD coating. 316L stainless steel does not contain lead or cadmium. The PVD coating materials (titanium nitride, zirconium nitride, chromium nitride) are biocompatible and non-toxic. We comply with EU REACH regulations (Registration, Evaluation, Authorization, and Restriction of Chemicals) and do not use any restricted substances in our jewelry. Your safety is our priority.

🔬 Material & Durability (Q9–Q15)

Q9: How long will the gold/rose gold color last?

Our earrings feature PVD (Physical Vapor Deposition) coating — a vacuum-based process where the color is created by forming a compound (e.g., titanium nitride for gold-tone, zirconium nitride for rose gold-tone) that bonds to the steel at the atomic level. Unlike traditional electroplating, PVD color is integral to the coating, not a separate surface layer. With normal daily wear and basic care, our PVD finish lasts 2–5 years before showing any noticeable wear. Avoid harsh chemicals (bleach, strong acids), abrasive cleaners, and swimming in heavily chlorinated pools to maximize color retention.

Q10: Will the earrings tarnish or rust?

316L stainless steel with PVD coating is highly resistant to tarnish and rust. 316L contains molybdenum, which provides exceptional corrosion resistance — the same reason it's used in marine equipment and surgical implants. The PVD coating adds an additional protective layer. Under normal conditions (daily wear, sweat, humidity, occasional rain exposure), our earrings will not tarnish or rust. Prolonged exposure to harsh chemicals (bleach, pool chlorine at industrial concentrations, strong acids) can eventually affect the finish, but these are extreme conditions far beyond normal use.

Q11: Can I wear these earrings while swimming or showering?

Yes, you can wear them while showering and in most water situations. The 316L base metal resists corrosion from fresh water, salt water, and perspiration. The PVD coating provides additional protection. However, we recommend removing jewelry before entering heavily chlorinated pools (commercial pool chlorine concentrations can be aggressive over time) or hot tubs, as prolonged exposure to high heat and chemicals can accelerate wear on any jewelry finish. Rinse with clean water and dry after saltwater exposure.

Q12: Will the earrings scratch easily?

PVD coatings are extremely hard — ranging from 2,200 to 4,500 HV (Vickers Hardness). For comparison, 24K gold is about 25–30 HV, and tool steel is about 800–900 HV. Our PVD coating is approximately 100 times harder than gold and significantly harder than most objects your earrings will encounter in daily life. While no material is completely scratch-proof (diamond is 10,000 HV), our PVD-coated earrings resist scratches far better than gold-plated, silver, or brass jewelry.

Q13: Are the earrings suitable for daily wear in the Philippines' humid climate?

Absolutely — they're designed for it. The Philippines' tropical climate (25–34°C, 70–90% humidity) is one of the most challenging environments for jewelry. Our earrings are specifically constructed for these conditions: (1) 316L stainless steel base resists pitting corrosion from humidity and sweat — something 304 steel cannot handle long-term. (2) PVD coating provides a dense, pinhole-free barrier that sweat and moisture cannot penetrate. (3) The molybdenum in 316L acts as a chloride-ion shield, preventing the salt corrosion that ruins lower-grade jewelry within months. Our earrings are tested to withstand the tropical stress test.

Q14: What gauge/thickness are the earring posts?

Our standard earring posts are 20 gauge (0.8mm), which is the most common size for fashion earrings and fits the vast majority of standard ear piercings. The posts are made from solid 316L stainless steel (not plated or filled) for maximum strength and hypoallergenic properties. If you need a different gauge for a specific piercing type, please check the product description or contact our customer service team.

Q15: How does 316L stainless steel compare to titanium for earrings?

Both are excellent choices for sensitive ears, with some differences: Titanium (specifically Grade 23/Ti-6Al-4V ELI) is slightly more biocompatible and lighter — it's the gold standard for medical implants. However, titanium is more expensive and less available in fashion jewelry styles. 316L stainless steel offers nearly equivalent biocompatibility (ASTM F138 certified for surgical implants), greater durability against bending/deformation, better PVD coating adhesion, and a significantly lower price point. For fashion earrings worn daily, 316L stainless steel provides the best balance of safety, durability, aesthetics, and value.

🧼 Care & Cleaning (Q16–Q21)

Q16: How should I clean my PVD-coated earrings?

Cleaning is simple: (1) Mix warm water with a few drops of mild, fragrance-free soap. (2) Soak the earrings for 2–3 minutes. (3) Gently clean with a soft microfiber cloth or a very soft-bristled brush (like a baby toothbrush). (4) Rinse thoroughly with clean water. (5) Dry completely with a soft, lint-free cloth before storing. Do NOT use: ultrasonic cleaners (can damage PVD at micro level), abrasive cleaners or scrub pads, bleach, ammonia, acetone, or jewelry dips containing harsh chemicals. Clean every 2–4 weeks for best results, or more often if worn during heavy exercise.

Q17: How should I store my earrings?

Store earrings in a dry, cool place away from direct sunlight. Best practices: (1) Keep each pair in a separate compartment or soft pouch to prevent scratching between pieces. (2) Use anti-tarnish strips or silica gel packets in your jewelry box to absorb moisture (especially important in the Philippines). (3) Store away from bathroom humidity — don't leave jewelry on the bathroom counter. (4) Remove earrings before applying perfume, hairspray, lotion, or sunscreen, and wait for products to dry before putting earrings back on. (5) Always store clean and completely dry.

Q18: Can I use a jewelry polishing cloth on PVD-coated earrings?

Yes, but use only microfiber or specifically labeled "PVD-safe" polishing cloths. Standard jewelry polishing cloths designed for silver or gold often contain mild abrasives or chemical cleaning agents that can dull the PVD surface over time. A clean, dry microfiber cloth is the safest option for routine polishing. Lightly buff in one direction to restore shine. Avoid excessive pressure — PVD is hard, but the surface can show micro-scratches if polished aggressively with the wrong material.

Q19: What should I avoid to keep my earrings looking new?

To maximize the lifespan of your earrings, avoid: (1) Direct contact with perfumes, hairsprays, lotions, and sunscreens — apply these products before putting on earrings and let them dry. (2) Swimming in heavily chlorinated pools or hot tubs. (3) Using ultrasonic jewelry cleaners. (4) Cleaning with bleach, ammonia, acetone, or alcohol-based products. (5) Wearing during heavy-contact sports or activities where the earrings might be struck or pulled. (6) Storing multiple pieces together without separation. (7) Exposure to mercury (found in some skin-lightening products) — mercury can rapidly damage gold-tone PVD coatings.

Q20: My earrings got exposed to saltwater/chlorine — what should I do?

Rinse immediately with clean, fresh water to remove salt or chlorine residue. Gently dry with a soft microfiber cloth. Salt crystals left on the surface can be mildly abrasive if allowed to dry and rub against the coating. Chlorine, while not immediately damaging to PVD at pool concentrations, can cause gradual degradation with repeated prolonged exposure. A quick freshwater rinse after beach or pool days is usually sufficient.

Q21: How often should I clean my earrings in the Philippine climate?

We recommend a quick wipe with a dry microfiber cloth after each wear (removes oils and sweat before they accumulate). A full gentle clean with mild soap and water every 2–4 weeks. If you wear earrings during exercise or spend a lot of time outdoors in the heat, increase the full cleaning frequency to every 1–2 weeks. The combination of high humidity, perspiration, and airborne salt (in coastal areas) means cleaning frequency should be higher in the Philippines than in cooler, drier climates.
