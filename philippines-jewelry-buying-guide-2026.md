## Part 1: Understanding Jewelry Materials (What Actually Touches Your Skin)

### Stainless Steel: Not All Steel Is Equal

The term "stainless steel" gets thrown around constantly in Philippine jewelry listings. But there's a critical difference:

| Grade | Nickel Content | Corrosion Resistance | Best For |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| **304 Stainless Steel** | 8-10.5% nickel | Good | Occasional wear, non-sensitive skin |
| **316L Stainless Steel** | 10-14% nickel (but molecularly bound) | Excellent | Daily wear, sensitive skin |
| **Surgical Steel** | Varies (no legal definition) | Unknown | Marketing term — not a real grade |

**The truth about "surgical steel":** There is no formal definition or standard for what qualifies as "surgical steel." In the Philippines, sellers use this term as a marketing shortcut that tells you nothing about the actual metal composition. Always look for the specific grade: 304 or 316L.

**Key insight:** 316L contains more nickel than 304, but its molecular structure binds the nickel so tightly that it doesn't leach onto skin. This is why 316L is actually safer for nickel-sensitive individuals despite having a higher nickel percentage on paper.

### Gold Plating vs PVD Coating: The Durability Difference

This is where most Filipino jewelry buyers get tricked:

| Coating Method | Thickness | Durability | Cost |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| **Flash Gold Plating** | <0.1 micron | 1-2 weeks | Cheapest |
| **Standard Gold Plating** | 0.5-1 micron | 1-3 months | Low-mid |
| **PVD (Physical Vapor Deposition)** | 1-3 microns | 1-3 years | Mid |
| **Solid Gold** | N/A (solid) | Lifetime | Most expensive |

**How to spot the trick:** Sellers who only say "gold plated" without specifying the method or thickness are almost always using the cheapest flash plating. In the Philippine climate (heat + humidity + sweat), flash plating can wear off within days.

Always ask: "Anong klaseng plating ito? PVD po ba o regular plating? Gaano kakapal ang coating?"

### Hypoallergenic vs Nickel-Free: The Label Trap

- **"Hypoallergenic"** is not a regulated term in the Philippines or internationally. Any brand can slap it on any product.
- **"Nickel-free"** should mean zero nickel, but testing is rarely done on budget jewelry.
- **The EU Nickel Directive (EN 1811)** sets a legal limit: jewelry must release less than 0.5 µg/cm²/week of nickel. This is the only reliable standard.

When buying hypoallergenic jewelry in the Philippines, look for:
1. Specific material grades (316L, Grade 23 Titanium, 14K+ gold)
2. PVD coating certification (at least 1 micron)
3. Brands that explain *why* their products are hypoallergenic, not just that they are

## Part 3: How to Spot Quality Jewelry (Even Online)

### The 5-Second Quality Check

Before adding to cart, check these five things:

1. **Material disclosure** — Does the listing specify the exact metal grade (e.g., "316L stainless steel") or just say "alloy" / "surgical steel" / "stainless"?
2. **Coating information** — Does it say "PVD" or "IP plating"? If it just says "gold color" or "gold plated" with no method, assume it will fade fast.
3. **Weight** — Legitimate listings often mention gram weight. If they don't, ask. Feather-light earrings at suspiciously low prices are almost always hollow/low-quality.
4. **Backing/clasp type** — Quality brands specify this (e.g., "titanium post with bullet back"). Generic "earring back" = red flag.
5. **Care instructions** — Brands that include care instructions (keep dry, avoid perfume, store separately) understand their product. Brands that don't mention care are drop-shipping.

### Red Flags in Philippine Jewelry Listings

- 🚩 "Alloy" without specifying which metals
- 🚩 "Surgical steel" as the only material description
- 🚩 "18K gold plated" at ₱99 — physically impossible at that price
- 🚩 "Hypoallergenic" on a ₱50 product with zero material details
- 🚩 Only showing product photos with heavy filters or in packaging (not worn)
- 🚩 No size specifications in millimeters
- 🚩 "Imported" as a quality claim (imported from where? China is also imported)

## Part 4: Jewelry Care in the Philippine Climate

The Philippine climate — hot, humid, and often rainy — is jewelry's worst enemy. Here's how to make your pieces last:

### Daily Care Routine

1. **Last on, first off.** Put jewelry on after perfume, lotion, and hairspray. Remove before showering, swimming, or exercising.
2. **Wipe after wear.** A quick wipe with a soft, dry microfiber cloth removes sweat and oils that accelerate tarnishing.
3. **Store separately.** Pieces rubbing against each other in a jewelry box cause micro-scratches. Use individual pouches or a compartmentalized organizer.
4. **Silica gel is your friend.** Place silica gel packets in your jewelry storage to absorb humidity. Replace every 2-3 months.

### Rainy Season Special Care (June-November)

- Never wear non-waterproof jewelry during heavy rain
- If jewelry gets wet, dry immediately with a soft cloth (don't air dry)
- Increase silica gel replacement to monthly
- Consider a dehumidifier for your jewelry storage area
- PVD-coated pieces handle humidity better than standard plated pieces

### When to Retire a Piece

- Green/discolored skin after wearing (nickel or copper leaching)
- Visible plating peeling or base metal showing
- Persistent irritation or itching (even if the piece was previously fine — metal can degrade)
- Bent or misshapen findings that can't be repaired

### The "Cost Per Wear" Mindset

Instead of looking at the purchase price, calculate cost per wear:

```
Cost Per Wear = Price ÷ Estimated Number of Wears
```

A ₱500 pair of earrings worn 3x/week for a year = ₱500 ÷ 156 wears = ₱3.20 per wear.
A ₱99 pair that tarnishes after 5 wears = ₱99 ÷ 5 = ₱19.80 per wear.

The "expensive" option is actually 6x cheaper per use.

## Part 6: Special Considerations for Sensitive Ears

If your ears react to most earrings, here's the hierarchy of safe materials:

1. **Implant-grade Titanium (Grade 23)** — The gold standard. Used in medical implants. Zero nickel.
2. **Niobium** — Pure element, completely biocompatible. Limited design options.
3. **14K+ Solid Gold** — 14K is 58.3% gold; 18K is 75%. Higher karat = less other metals = safer.
4. **316L Stainless Steel with PVD** — Safe for most, but not all, sensitive individuals. The PVD coating creates a barrier.
5. **Platinum** — Hypoallergenic but priced for investment buyers.

**Materials to avoid if you have nickel sensitivity:**
- "Fashion jewelry" / "costume jewelry" (almost always nickel-based)
- White gold (often alloyed with nickel for the white color)
- Brass or copper-based alloys
- Anything labeled only as "alloy" or "mixed metal"

## Part 7: Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Why do my ears turn green when I wear earrings?
The green discoloration is caused by copper in the metal reacting with your sweat and skin oils. It's not dangerous, but it means the jewelry contains copper or brass. Look for stainless steel, titanium, or solid gold to avoid this.

### Q: Is "surgical steel" safe for sensitive ears?
There's no legal definition for "surgical steel." Some surgical steel is 316L and safe; some is 304 or lower grades and can cause reactions. Always ask for the specific stainless steel grade.

### Q: How long should PVD-coated jewelry last in the Philippines?
With proper care, 1-3 years. PVD coating is more durable than standard plating, but the Philippine climate (heat, humidity, sweat) accelerates wear. Daily wipe-downs and avoiding water exposure can extend lifespan significantly.

### Q: Can I shower with stainless steel jewelry?
316L stainless steel is water-resistant but not waterproof. Occasional water contact is usually fine, but frequent exposure to water, soap, and shampoo will degrade the finish faster. It's best to remove jewelry before showering.

### Q: Where can I buy genuine hypoallergenic jewelry in the Philippines?
Look for brands that specify their material grades (316L, titanium, etc.) and coating methods (PVD). Available through Shopee, Lazada, Instagram shops, and select physical stores. Avoid listings that use "hypoallergenic" as a buzzword without technical details.

### Q: What's the best jewelry metal for the Philippine climate?
316L stainless steel with PVD coating offers the best balance of durability, hypoallergenic properties, and affordability for the Philippine climate. Titanium is the safest for extreme sensitivities but costs more and has fewer design options.

### Q: Why do some ₱300 earrings last longer than ₱800 ones?
Price doesn't always equal quality. A ₱300 pair from a brand that uses 316L + PVD can outlast an ₱800 pair from a brand using flash-plated brass. Always check materials, not just the price tag.
