Part 1: Understanding Jewelry Materials

Stainless Steel: Not All Steel Is Equal

GradeNickel ContentCorrosion ResistanceBest For
304 Stainless Steel8-10.5% nickelGoodOccasional wear, non-sensitive skin
316L Stainless Steel10-14% nickel (molecularly bound)ExcellentDaily wear, sensitive skin
Surgical SteelVaries (no legal definition)UnknownMarketing term — not a real grade

Key insight: 316L contains more nickel than 304, but its molecular structure binds the nickel so tightly that it doesn't leach onto skin. This is why 316L is actually safer for nickel-sensitive individuals.

Hypoallergenic vs Nickel-Free

  • "Hypoallergenic" is not a regulated term in the Philippines or internationally.
  • "Nickel-free" should mean zero nickel, but testing is rarely done on budget jewelry.
  • The EU Nickel Directive (EN 1811) sets the only reliable standard: <0.5 µg/cm²/week nickel release.

Part 3: How to Spot Quality Jewelry

The 5-Second Quality Check

  1. Material disclosure — Exact metal grade or just "alloy"?
  2. Coating information — "PVD" or just "gold plated"?
  3. Weight — Gram weight listed?
  4. Backing/clasp type — Specified or generic?
  5. Care instructions — Provided or absent?

Red Flags

  • 🚩 "Alloy" without specifying which metals
  • 🚩 "Surgical steel" as the only material description
  • 🚩 "18K gold plated" at ₱99 — physically impossible
  • 🚩 "Hypoallergenic" on a ₱50 product with zero material details

Part 4: Jewelry Care in the Philippine Climate

  1. Last on, first off. Put jewelry on after perfume, lotion. Remove before showering, swimming.
  2. Wipe after wear. Dry microfiber cloth removes sweat and oils.
  3. Store separately. Use individual pouches or compartmentalized organizers.
  4. Silica gel packets absorb humidity. Replace every 2-3 months.
